How it works

From radiograph to explanation in three steps

Every image goes through the same preprocessing the model saw during validation, so the scores here match the numbers it was evaluated on.

01

Upload

Drop in a frontal (PA or AP) chest X-ray as PNG or JPEG. It is converted to greyscale, resized and centre-cropped to 320 px.

02

Score 14 findings

A DenseNet-121 outputs an independent score per finding. Each is compared with its own threshold, tuned on held-out validation data.

03

Explain with Grad-CAM

For any finding, Grad-CAM traces the score back to the final feature map and highlights the regions that pushed it up.

Performance

How well it separates each finding

AUROC is the probability that the model scores a random positive image above a random negative one. AUPRC is harsher on rare findings, because its baseline is the finding's prevalence.

What it looks for

Limitations

  • Research and education only. This is not a medical device and must not inform any clinical decision.
  • Noisy labels. NLP-mined labels are roughly 90% accurate, which caps every metric shown.
  • Scores are not probabilities. Class weighting inflates them; read each against its own threshold.
  • Heatmaps show attention, not lesions. Grad-CAM is coarse (one cell ≈ 32 px) and can highlight confounders such as tubes, text markers or the AP/PA projection.
  • One hospital, frontal views. Trained on NIH Clinical Center images; lateral views, children and other scanners are out of distribution.
